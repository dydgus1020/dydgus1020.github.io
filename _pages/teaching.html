Teaching Philosophy

I believe that effective learning begins by connecting new ideas to what students already know. When introducing a new mathematical concept, I prefer to first recall relevant prior knowledge, present a problem or limitation, and then introduce the new concept as a natural way to move forward. Intuition provides a map for this process, but I ultimately place greater value on precise definitions, rigorous reasoning, and proofs. My goal is for students to understand not only each step of an argument, but also why that step is necessary and where it fits into the overall logic.

I distinguish conceptual understanding from the ability to apply a concept. A student who truly understands a concept should be able to explain its definition, distinguish it from related ideas, and respond to questions about it. Application, on the other hand, requires practice and the gradual development of problem-solving judgment. In evaluation, I therefore value the direction of reasoning and consistent effort rather than correctness alone, recognizing that students learn at different speeds.

I also encourage the use of computational and AI tools, but I believe that students should first develop enough foundational knowledge to understand and critically evaluate their outputs. Above all, I hope to be an approachable instructor who encourages questions and creates an environment where students feel comfortable admitting what they do not yet understand.
